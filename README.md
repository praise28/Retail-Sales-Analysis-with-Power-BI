# Retail Sales Dashboard — Power BI

## Project Overview

This project is an interactive **Retail Sales Dashboard** built with Microsoft Power BI. The dashboard is designed to monitor sales performance, profitability, products, locations, customers, and salespeople from a retail sales dataset.

The report presents key performance indicators (KPIs) and interactive visuals that make it easier to identify sales trends and compare performance across different business dimensions.

## Business Problem

Retail businesses generate transactional data across products, locations, customers, and salespeople, but this data is often scattered across multiple spreadsheets and difficult to interpret without consolidation. Without a unified view, it is hard to answer basic but critical questions:

- Is revenue growth actually translating into higher profit, or is it eroding margins?
- Which states/locations are underperforming and may need attention?
- Which products drive strong profit versus those that sell well but contribute little margin?
- How are Premium and Standard sales segments contributing to overall performance?
- Which salespeople are outperforming or underperforming, and where might coaching help?

This dashboard was built to turn raw, disconnected retail sales data into a single interactive view that lets stakeholders answer these questions quickly and make informed decisions on pricing, inventory, regional strategy, and sales support.

## Data & Methodology

1. **Source data:** A sample retail sales dataset was sourced online, consisting of several related Excel worksheets (e.g. sales transactions, products, locations, customers, salespeople).
2. **Data cleaning:** Each worksheet was cleaned in Excel — resolving inconsistent formatting, removing duplicates/blank rows, and standardizing column names and data types so the tables could relate to one another cleanly.
3. **Import into Power BI:** The cleaned worksheets were imported into Power BI Desktop as separate tables.
4. **Data modeling:** A relational data model was built in Power BI, connecting the Customers, Products, Locations, Sales People, and Retail Sales tables through appropriate relationships (primarily via key/ID fields) to support cross-filtering and aggregation across the report.
5. **DAX measures:** Key business measures (Total Revenue, Total Cost, Profit, Profit Margin, Premium Sales, Standard Sales) were created using DAX on top of the model.
6. **Dashboard design:** Interactive visuals and a product slicer were built on top of the model to present KPIs, trends, and comparisons across time, geography, products, and salespeople.

## Insights & Recommendations

### Key Insights

**1. Revenue and profitability should be monitored together.**
The dashboard tracks Total Revenue, Total Cost, Profit, and Profit Margin as separate KPIs, making it possible to distinguish sales growth from genuinely profitable growth. Strong revenue performance should not automatically be read as strong business performance — revenue should be evaluated alongside profit and margin when assessing products, salespeople, and periods.

**2. Revenue changes over time.**
The time-series visual compares monthly revenue and profit across years, allowing performance and profitability changes to be tracked together. Periods of rising revenue can be checked against profit trends to see whether growth is actually improving profitability.

**3. Geographic performance varies by state.**
The state-level revenue visual shows that revenue is not evenly distributed geographically, which helps identify high-performing markets and locations that may need additional sales activity.

**4. Product performance is an important driver of profit.**
Comparing product names against profit makes it possible to identify products that contribute strongly to profitability versus those with comparatively weaker returns — product volume alone shouldn't drive inventory or promotional priorities.

**5. Premium and Standard sales are tracked separately.**
Dedicated KPIs for Premium and Standard sales provide a basis for evaluating the contribution of higher-value versus standard sales and understanding the composition of overall revenue.

**6. Salesperson performance can be compared directly.**
Comparing salesperson names with total revenue helps identify performance differences, recognize strong contributors, and flag areas where additional support or coaching may help.

### Recommendations

1. **Monitor revenue and profit together** — use profit and profit margin alongside revenue when evaluating business growth.
2. **Investigate geographic differences** — prioritize deeper analysis of high- and low-performing states.
3. **Optimize the product portfolio** — review products with strong revenue but weak profit to check pricing, cost, or discounting.
4. **Use segment-level reporting** — track Premium and Standard sales separately to understand shifts in sales mix and customer value.
5. **Use salesperson analytics for targeted support** — combine revenue performance with product and geographic information before making decisions.
6. **Extend the analysis with profitability trends** — regularly compare revenue growth against profit-margin movement to catch growth that's coming at the expense of margins.

### Analytical Approach

- **Business KPIs:** Revenue, Cost, Profit, Profit Margin
- **Time analysis:** Revenue and Profit by year/month
- **Geographic analysis:** Revenue by state
- **Product analysis:** Profit by product
- **Sales analysis:** Revenue by salesperson
- **Segmentation:** Premium vs Standard sales
- **Interactive filtering:** Product-level filtering through the dashboard slicer

### Business Value

The dashboard transforms transactional retail data into an interactive management view that supports sales monitoring, profitability analysis, product evaluation, regional analysis, and salesperson performance review.
